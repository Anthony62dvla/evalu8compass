DPIA information for schools

What your school needs to complete its own data protection impact assessment (DPIA), or to record why it doesn't need a full one.

Last reviewed {{UPDATED}}

Your school is the controller of what it records in Evalu8 Compass, so the DPIA decision is your school's. This page sets out how the service works, the risks we've identified and how we reduce them. Your data protection officer is welcome to ask us anything: support@evalu8compass.app.

1. The processing

PurposeSchool self-evaluation against Ofsted's school inspection framework and related standards; collecting anonymous staff views in workshops; planning and tracking improvement actions; optional AI improvement suggestions.
NatureLeaders rate expectations, record evidence notes and actions, and save termly snapshots. Staff answer the same expectations anonymously in a facilitated workshop and the results are collated. Optional AI features generate suggestions from the text sent to them.
ScopeOne school's leadership team and staff. Personal data is limited: mostly job roles, plus any names leaders choose to record as action owners or report authors.
ContextUsed by school leaders for internal evaluation. Staff take part in workshops voluntarily and anonymously. Not designed for pupils' personal information.
Our roleProcessor for your school's content (terms in our terms of use). Controller only for access-code contact details, support emails and security logs.
Lawful basis (your school's)Typically public task for maintained schools and academies, and legitimate interests for independent schools, as part of the school's duty to evaluate and improve its provision. Confirm this with your DPO.

3. Necessity and proportionality

  • Data minimisation: workshops collect roles, not identities. The self-evaluation is about the school, not individuals.
  • AI is optional: it can be switched off for the whole school, and then nothing is sent to the AI provider. When it's on, it runs only when someone presses an AI button, and only the text needed for that request is sent.
  • Access control: each school's information is only reachable with that school's access code.
  • No secondary use: no advertising, analytics, profiling or AI training.
  • Transparency: staff see a privacy note before answering, and our privacy notice is linked throughout.

4. How data flows

  1. A leader's or staff member's browser sends information over HTTPS to our server in {{HOSTING}}.
  2. The server stores each school's information separately. A copy of the self-evaluation is also kept in the browser of each device that opens it, for offline use.
  3. Only if AI is used: the text for that request goes to Anthropic PBC (US) over HTTPS. The result comes back and is shown to the user. Anthropic doesn't train on it and deletes it within 30 days.
  4. Every day, an encrypted backup (AES-256) is made on the server and copied to {{BACKUP}}.
  5. Leaders can download reports (PDF or Word) to their own devices. These are then under your school's control.

6. Security measures

  • HTTPS everywhere with HSTS. A strict Content Security Policy blocks scripts and fonts from other sites, and there are no third-party scripts, fonts or trackers.
  • Access codes are stored only as SHA-256 hashes. Sign-in cookies are signed (HMAC), HttpOnly and Secure.
  • Each school's information is kept in its own store and is only reachable with its own code. Cross-site requests are refused.
  • Rate limiting on sign-in, on AI use and on the API in general.
  • The developer's superadmin console needs a password (scrypt-hashed) plus an authenticator app code. Its sessions last 8 hours and every action is logged. The console shows counts only, never school content.
  • The app runs as its own unprivileged system user, with read-only access to its code and write access only to its data folder. The AI key is held only on the server and is never sent to browsers.
  • Daily encrypted backups (AES-256 with a key held only by the operator), with off-site copies and restore checks.
  • An automatic health check restarts the service if it stops responding.

8. What your school should record

  • Whether a full DPIA is needed. Given the limited personal data and the measures above, many schools record a screening decision instead. This is your DPO's call.
  • Your lawful basis, and whether you'll allow AI suggestions.
  • How you'll brief staff: keep names out of notes and comments, and explain that workshops are anonymous.
  • Who in your school holds the access code, and how you'll keep that list up to date.
  • Your DPO's advice and the decision-maker's sign-off, with a review date (we suggest yearly, or when the service changes significantly).
