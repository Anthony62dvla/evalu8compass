Section 1

{{ICON:compass}}What Evalu8 Compass is for

Evalu8 Compass helps leaders and staff answer one question honestly, how well are we doing?, and then keep improving all year, not just before an inspection.

It sets out what inspectors look for under Ofsted's renewed school inspection framework, matched to your school's type (maintained, academy or independent), your phases (EYFS to post-16) and any specialist provision. You rate each expectation, record your evidence, hear from every member of staff, and turn what you find into a plan you can act on.

Then it keeps the plan live: it reminds leaders by email, records your monitoring and the DSL's safeguarding checks, writes the agenda for each half-termly leadership meeting, and produces reports for subject and area leaders, your school improvement partner and your governors.

{{ICON:users-group}} Built for co-creation

Self-evaluation is strongest when it is shared. Leaders rate the school, staff rate the same expectations anonymously, and the differences become the most useful conversations.

{{ICON:sparkles}} AI is your choice

Everything works without AI. If you switch it on, an AI "inspector" suggests improvement ideas and drafts plans. It only runs when you press an AI button, and it never changes your ratings.

{{ICON:alert-triangle}}

Advisory, not a judgement. Ratings are your own, and AI suggestions are advice. Nothing in Evalu8 Compass is an Ofsted judgement or predicts one. Always check current guidance: requirements change.

Section 2

{{ICON:lock}}Getting started: your access code

Your school has one access code, in the form ABCD-EFGH-JKLM. It opens your school's checklist, action plan, progress, reports and workshops. Your first term is free.

  1. Go to evalu8compass.app, choose Start your free term and fill in the short form. Your code arrives by email straight away.
  2. Choose Sign in on the front page.
  3. Type your access code (capitals and dashes don't matter) and choose Enter.
  4. You stay signed in on that device for 30 days. Use Sign out in the top bar on a shared computer.

One copy for your whole team

Everything is saved to your school's account. Everyone who signs in with your code sees and edits the same copy, on any device. Two people can work at once: changes to different lines are kept, and colleagues' changes appear within about 20 seconds.

The badge at the top of the checklist shows whether your work is saved: Saved to your school's account, Saving…, or Offline: saved on this device. If the connection drops, keep working; changes are sent when it comes back.

{{ICON:lock}}

Treat the code like a password. Share it only with the leaders who need it. If it is passed on by mistake, email support@evalu8compass.app: we'll replace it straight away and your work is kept.

Lost it? Use Lost your code? on the sign-in page and a new one is emailed to the address you signed up with.

Staff never need the code. They join workshops with a separate 6-character workshop code.

Governors and trustees get their own read-only code (section 12). It never opens the checklist.

Section 3 continued

Step 2: upload your latest Ofsted report

Straight after your school's details, upload your most recent Ofsted report. Choose Choose PDF (or paste the report's text), then Read the report. The AI reads it once and saves:

  • the inspection date, the framework, the safeguarding outcome and each area's grade;
  • the report's next steps, in order;
  • which checklist lines the report raised. These lines are marked Ofsted concern (or Ofsted strength) in the checklist.

You only do this once. The AI inspector's advice and readiness review take the report into account, it appears in your self-evaluation report, and every staff workshop you start offers to use it.

New independent schools

If your school is independent and hasn't yet had its first standard inspection, tick New school in Step 1 and enter the month you opened. Then:

  • In Step 2, upload your pre-registration inspection report. The AI reads which standards were judged likely or unlikely to be met, and flags anything unlikely to be met on your checklist.
  • A Ready for your first standard inspection? panel lists what must be up and running, in order: before pupils start, the first half-term, by the end of the first term, and before the inspection. Each item shows its status from your ratings, with the standard it comes from.
  • First inspection readiness gives an AI evaluation against each part of the independent school standards, four priorities, and the evidence of implementation to build up. Your first standard inspection comes within your first year of operation.
{{ICON:alert-triangle}}

The reading is done by AI. Check the grades and next steps against your report. Reading it uses one AI request and needs AI suggestions switched on in Step 1. No report yet, for example a new school? Skip this step.

Section 4

{{ICON:list-check}}Rate the checklist

Each evaluation area lists what inspectors look for. Rate every line and note your evidence.

RatingUse it when
MetYou can evidence it consistently, across the school.
PartlyIt's in place but not consistent, or not yet embedded.
Not yetIt's a gap.
N/AIt genuinely doesn't apply to your school.
{{ICON:bulb}}

Be honest, and evidence-led. "Partly" with a clear note is more useful than a generous "Met". Inspectors will test what you say.

Keep names out of notes. Describe what happens, not who: "some subject staff log concerns late", not a colleague's name. Never record information about individual pupils.

Useful tools

  • Not yet assessed and Gaps only filter the list.
  • Collapse all folds the areas so you can work one at a time.
  • Save / load copies your whole self-evaluation as text, as an extra backup.
Section 5

{{ICON:sparkles}}The AI inspector (optional)

Two kinds of help, both advisory. Use them only if your school has AI switched on.

Ideas for one line

Rate a line Partly or Not yet and choose Ask the AI inspector. You get 3–4 activities ordered from quickest to longest, each with a lead, a timescale and the evidence it will produce, plus the question an inspector is likely to ask. Choose Add to action plan on any activity you want to keep.

Inspector review of the whole checklist

Choose Inspector review for indicative grades for each area, four priorities for the coming term, strengths to showcase, and a separate list of compliance checks (policies or statutory items to fix now).

{{ICON:sparkles}}

Monthly allowance. Each school has a monthly allowance of AI requests, shown under the AI switch. If it runs out, you'll see built-in suggestions until it resets on the 1st of the month. Everything else keeps working.

Section 6

{{ICON:target}}Build your action plan

Open the Action plan tab. Every action has an owner, a deadline and what success looks like.

Three ways to add actions

  • Add an action to write your own.
  • Add actions for my gaps creates one for every line rated Partly or Not yet.
  • Draft actions with AI drafts a plan from your gaps for you to edit.

Keep it moving

  • Set each action to Not started, In progress or Done.
  • Overdue actions are flagged. Use the Overdue filter before a leadership meeting.
  • Link an action to a checklist line so reports show them together.
  • Mark each action On track, At risk or Off track. At risk and off track are emailed to key leaders straight away.
  • Break bigger actions into milestones with dates; they appear in each owner's Monday email.
  • When an action is done, record its impact: what staff, pupils or an inspector now see.

Actions also arrive from other places, labelled with where they came from: From leadership meeting, From monitoring and From governors.

Section 7

{{ICON:users}}Your leadership team and automatic emails

Add your leaders once in the Team tab. Evalu8 Compass then keeps them informed, so nothing slips.

  1. Start with the headteacher, deputy and designated safeguarding lead, then the leaders who own actions (SENCo, attendance lead, subject leaders). Add governors or trustees, and your school improvement partner (role: School improvement partner or consultant) too.
  2. Use Send a test email to check each address works.
  3. An action's Owner can be a name or a role (for example "DSL"); alerts go to whoever it matches.

Section 8

{{ICON:eye}}Monitoring and the DSL's safeguarding checks

The Monitoring tab is where leaders plan and record what they check, and what they find.

The DSL's termly safeguarding checks

Ofsted expects leaders to know that safeguarding works in practice for pupils, not just on paper. Eight checks are set out each term, each with prompts for what to ask or look at: staff safeguarding knowledge, pupil voice on feeling safe, a records audit, filtering and monitoring, pupils who need help or protection, absence and children missing education, training and induction, and governor oversight. (The single central record stays in your MIS.)

Section 8 continued

Recording any monitoring

  1. Choose Record monitoring (something that's happened) or Plan monitoring (something in the calendar; whoever is carrying it out is reminded in their Monday email).
  2. Choose the type (learning walk, lesson drop-in, work scrutiny, pupil, staff or parent voice, records review, governor visit), the date, who carried it out and the evaluation area. If it's evidence for a particular checklist line, choose it.
  3. Write What we found and What needs to happen next, then the overall judgement: Working well, Developing or Concern.
  4. Add to the line's evidence copies your findings into that line's evidence notes in Evaluate. Turn next steps into an action adds it to the improvement plan.
{{ICON:alert-triangle}}

Write about what you saw across the school. Don't record pupils' or staff names. A Concern in safeguarding is emailed to the head, deputy and DSL straight away; elsewhere, to the head and that area's leader.

Monitoring feeds the leadership meeting agenda, each area's report, the SIP discussion paper and the governors' report.

Section 9

{{ICON:calendar-check}}Half-termly leadership meetings

The Meetings tab writes each agenda from what your plan and self-evaluation show, and turns decisions into actions.

  1. Choose Plan this year's meetings for six half-termly dates (check they suit your term dates), or Schedule a meeting for one. The head, deputy, DSL, assistant heads and SENCo are invited by default.
  2. Choose Write the agenda. It puts safeguarding first, then actions at risk or overdue, what has changed since the last meeting, what monitoring shows, areas needing attention, lines rated Not yet with no action, and the impact of your work. Add your own items too.
  3. A week before, the agenda is emailed to attendees automatically. You can also Email the agenda now.
Section 9 continued

In the meeting

Choose Start the meeting. For each item you can record what was decided, agree ratings for the lines it covers, and add new actions with an owner and date: they go straight into the improvement plan.

Choose Finish and send the minutes. The minutes, with each decision and new action, are emailed to attendees, and a snapshot of your self-evaluation is saved so the next agenda can show what has changed.

Section 10

{{ICON:file-text}}Leaders' reports and subject reviews

The Reports tab has one report for each of Ofsted's evaluation areas, owned by its leader.

  1. Choose Open report and tick who leads the area.
  2. Enter the key figures for this term and last (for example attendance, persistent absence, suspensions, safeguarding concerns logged). Whole-school numbers only, never pupils' names.
  3. Write the leader's commentary: where are we, what are we doing about it, and what difference is it making?
  4. The report adds what isn't yet in place, strengths with evidence, actions and their impact, monitoring this term and what has changed. Print or save as PDF, or Email to the leader and head.
Section 10 continued

Subject reviews

In Curriculum and teaching, add a review for each subject from the list. Each subject leader rates six questions (sequencing, teaching, assessment, pupils' work, SEND and disadvantaged pupils, staff expertise) and records strengths and next steps. The reviews appear as a table in the curriculum report.

Section 11

{{ICON:briefcase}}SIP and consultant visits

Before your school improvement partner or consultant visits, Evalu8 Compass writes a discussion paper for the meeting with the head and senior leaders.

  1. Add your adviser in the Team tab with the role School improvement partner or consultant (external).
  2. In Meetings, choose Plan a SIP or consultant visit. The head, deputy and your adviser are invited.
  3. Write What we'd like from this visit. Choose Read the discussion paper to check it: the school, your self-evaluation at a glance, your latest Ofsted report, priorities and how they're going, impact, monitoring, safeguarding, what has changed, and questions for discussion.
  4. A week before the visit, your leaders (not the adviser) are emailed the draft to check. When you're happy, choose Email the discussion paper.
  5. On the day, the visit's agenda follows the paper. Record decisions and actions as in any meeting.
{{ICON:lock}}

The discussion paper never includes evidence notes, pupils' information or what safeguarding checks found: only how many have been done.

Section 12

{{ICON:building-community}}Governors and trustees

At the foot of the Reports tab: the headteacher's termly report, governors' read-only access, and the challenge log.

The headteacher's termly report

The report is drafted for you from the live data: your self-evaluation compared with the last report, safeguarding (including the DSL's checks), leaders' key figures, priorities and impact, monitoring, your last inspection's next steps, answers to governors' questions, and suggested questions for governors to ask. Add your commentary, then choose Issue to governors. The issued copy is fixed, so governors read exactly what you issued. Then choose Email to governors.

Section 12 continued

Governors' access

  1. Add your governors or trustees, with their emails, in the Team tab.
  2. Choose Create a governors' code, with Email it to the governors and trustees ticked. The code is shown once.
  3. Governors sign in at evalu8compass.app with that code. They see issued reports, The school today, and Questions and answers. They can't change anything or see the checklist.
  4. If the code is shared by mistake, choose Replace the code: everyone using the old one is signed out. See what governors see opens their view.
Section 12 continued

The challenge log

Ofsted looks for governors holding leaders to account. When a governor asks a question in their view, it arrives in the challenge log and the head and deputy are emailed straight away. Write the answer and choose Send the answer: governors see it in their view, and it's included in the next termly report. You can also Make it an action, and Record challenge from a meeting or visit so challenge given in governors' meetings is kept in the same place.

{{ICON:building-community}} For governors and trustees

  1. Go to evalu8compass.app, choose Sign in and enter the governors' code your headteacher sent you.
  2. Termly reports: read the headteacher's reports, print or save them as PDF.
  3. The school today: where the school stands now, between reports.
  4. Questions and answers: ask a question, with your name; leaders are told straight away. Their answers appear here.

Everything is read-only and contains no information about individual pupils. Please keep the code within the board, and don't include pupils' or staff names in questions.

Section 13

{{ICON:building-community}}Church schools: SIAMS

For Church of England and Methodist schools: the Statutory Inspection of Anglican and Methodist Schools, alongside Ofsted, in the same place.

SIAMS asks whether, through its theologically rooted Christian vision, the school is living up to its foundation as a Church school and enabling pupils and adults to flourish. There are no criteria to tick off: for each Inspection Question, leaders show the impact, with evidence. Evalu8 Compass follows the SIAMS Framework (September 2024, updated September 2026).

  1. In Step 1, choose Church of England, Methodist or Joint under Church school, then your designation. A SIAMS tab appears after Evaluate.
  2. Inspection Question 7 (the quality of RE) appears for voluntary aided schools and former voluntary aided academies, and for former voluntary controlled academies that tick we teach denominational RE.
  3. Upload your last SIAMS report and choose Read the report (one AI request). The date, outcome and areas for development are filled in, and each finding is shown under its Inspection Question.
Section 13 continued

Where we stand

Telling your story

  • Who are we? Your factual context: pupils, leadership, partnerships. No judgement is made on it.
  • What are we doing here? Your vision, how it is theologically rooted, how your community's needs shape it, and your arrangements for governance, worship and RE. Write these in your own words: inspectors look for a vision leaders genuinely own, so Evalu8 Compass won't write it for you.
Section 13 continued

The Inspection Questions

Each question is shown as the framework asks it, with prompts to think about. Write your evidence of impact (what difference it makes, for whom, and how you know) and what isn't yet true, then choose whether you can answer it Yes, with evidence, Partly or Not yet. If you choose Partly or Not yet, you'll see what a SIAMS report would say. Use Add an action to put it in your improvement plan.

{{ICON:eye}}

Evidence from monitoring. In the Monitoring tab, record Collective worship and RE lesson visits, and link any record to a SIAMS Inspection Question. It appears under that question as evidence.

Section 13 continued

Your SIAMS self-evaluation

Choose Read the self-evaluation. It is written from everything above, in the framework's order: who we are, what we are doing here, each Inspection Question with its evidence and linked monitoring, and where you stand. Print or save as PDF, or Email to the head and our adviser (your diocesan adviser, added in the Team tab).

SIAMS also appears in your leadership meeting agendas ("Living our Christian vision"), the governors' termly report, and the discussion paper for your diocesan adviser's visit.

Section 15

{{ICON:file-export}}Exporting your self-evaluation report

Choose Export on the checklist tabs for a formatted self-evaluation report with your logo.

  • Tick what to include: lines rated Met, lines not yet assessed, evidence notes, the action plan, the AI review, and progress since a snapshot.

Section 16

{{ICON:presentation}}Running a staff workshop

Staff rate the school anonymously on their own phones. You see the collated findings live, then turn them into an improvement plan.

Before: start the workshop

  1. Sign in with your access code, then open Staff workshop.
  2. Under Run a workshop, check your school's name, type and phases. They're filled in from your checklist.
  3. Under Latest Ofsted report, keep Use your saved report ticked, or choose a different PDF for this workshop.
  4. Choose the areas to cover. Two or three areas suit a 45–60 minute session; the page shows how many lines that is.
  5. Choose Start workshop.

Suggested 60-minute session

5 minWhy we're doing this. Answers are anonymous, there are no wrong answers, and no names in comments.
20 minStaff join and rate the lines on their phones.
15 minShare findings. Look at where staff agree and where views differ.
15 minDiscuss the splits and gaps; agree ratings together.
5 minWhat happens next, and when staff will see the plan.
Section 16 continued

During: staff join and rate

  • Share findings with staff sends the collated results to everyone's phone when you're ready to discuss them. Present mode enlarges the findings for the projector.
  • Close responses stops new answers when you've finished rating.
  • Copy facilitator link lets you reopen the workshop on another device. Keep this link private: it gives full control of the workshop.

Live findings

{{ICON:users}}

Anonymity is built in. No names or accounts. A role's breakdown (for example "Teaching assistants") is only shown when at least three people in that role have answered. Comments are shown without names.

Section 16 continued

After: agree ratings and generate the plan

  1. Agree ratings. On each line choose the agreed rating after discussion, and add a note. Agree most common answers fills any you haven't agreed with the staff's most common answer: check these before relying on them.
  2. Open the Improvement plan tab and complete the four steps:
    • About your setting: pupil numbers and needs, recent changes, current priorities. No names.
    • Latest Ofsted report: already filled in if you used your saved report. Otherwise Choose PDF (or paste the text), then Read the report.
    • Summarise staff comments groups comments into themes.
    • Generate plan.
Section 16 continued

The improvement plan

The plan has four priorities for the coming term, led by your Ofsted report's next steps and where staff and leaders agree. Each priority is tagged with the evidence behind it (ofsted, staff, agreed, context) and has 2–3 actions with a lead, a date and the evidence each will produce. Policy and statutory gaps are listed separately as compliance checks to fix now. You also get quick wins and a short message to share with staff.

Then

  • Share plan with staff shows the priorities and your message on staff phones.
  • Copy plan copies it as text for your school development plan.
  • On the Export tab, Send to our checklist adds the agreed ratings (with the staff split as an evidence note) and the plan's actions to your school's checklist and action plan.
  • The Workshop report gives participation, findings, where views differ, themes, the Ofsted reading and the plan, as PDF or Word.

{{ICON:users-group}}Joining a staff workshop

It takes about 20 minutes, and it's anonymous.

  1. On your phone, scan the QR code on the screen, or go to evalu8compass.app and type the 6-character code.
  2. Choose your role: teacher, teaching assistant, support staff, senior leader, governor/trustee or other.
  3. Choose Join. No name, email or account is needed.
  4. For each line choose Met Partly Not yet or Don't know, from your own experience.
  5. Add a comment if you like: what's working, what isn't, an example.
{{ICON:lock}}

Your answers are anonymous. Your role is only shown when at least three people with that role have answered.

Please don't name pupils or colleagues in comments. Describe what happens, not who.

Answers save as you go. You can change them until the facilitator closes responses. When findings or the plan are shared, they appear on your phone.

Section 18

{{ICON:shield-check}}Your data and privacy

  • Your school controls its information. Only people with your school's access code can see your self-evaluation, action plan and snapshots.
  • Emails go only to the people your school lists in the Team tab. They never include evidence notes or what monitoring found.
  • Your adviser gets only the discussion paper you send, which has no evidence notes or pupils' information.
  • Governors and trustees have a separate read-only code. They see issued reports, a summary of where the school stands and their questions with your answers, never the checklist or your notes.
  • Workshops are anonymous. Staff give a role only. Exports and reports contain collated findings, never individual responses.
  • AI is optional and runs only when someone presses an AI button. The AI provider (Anthropic) doesn't train on your information and deletes it within 30 days.
  • No advertising, analytics or tracking. Everything runs from our own server over an encrypted connection, with daily encrypted backups.
  • Retention: workshops are deleted 90 days after they were last used. Your school's saved work is kept while you have access, then deleted. Ask us at any time to delete it sooner.

For your data protection officer, the DPIA information for schools page at evalu8compass.app/dpia.html sets out how the service works, the risks and how they're reduced. See also the privacy notice, terms and accessibility statement, linked at the foot of every page.
